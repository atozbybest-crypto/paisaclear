How it works

National Savings Certificate (NSC) is a government schemes product from Government of India — small savings scheme, in the guaranteed category: fixed by contract. A five-year certificate that compounds interest annually and pays the whole amount at maturity.

Because the return type is guaranteed, the questions that matter most are: Is the rate fixed for the whole tenure, or revised during it? Who is actually promising to pay, and what happens if they don't?

  • A five-year, tax-deductible commitment with a known end date
  • Collateral for a loan without selling the investment

Resident individuals, either alone or jointly. A guardian can invest for a minor.
Getting your money back
No premature encashment in normal circumstances. The certificate can be pledged as security for a loan.
Protection
A sovereign liability of the Government of India.
Tax
Qualifies for a deduction under Section 80C. Interest accrues each year and is taxable in that year even though it is paid at maturity — the accrued interest itself is treated as reinvested.

What can go wrong

  • Interest is taxed as it accrues, so you pay tax on money you have not received yet.
  • The five-year lock-in is rigid; exit is not normally possible.
  • The rate is fixed for the certificate, so later issues may offer more.

Before you decide anything

  • How the accrued interest is reported in your tax return
  • Whether the certificate is in single or joint names, and who it pays out to

These are questions to take to the provider, not a checklist that makes a product suitable. Whether something fits your situation is a decision only you — or an adviser you choose — can make.
