How it works

National Pension System (NPS) is a government schemes product from Government of India — small savings, in the market-linked category: no promised return. A voluntary pension account where contributions are invested in market-linked funds until you retire.

Because the return type is market-linked, the questions that matter most are: What exactly does this hold, and what would make it fall? Can I leave this money invested if it drops 30%?

  • Building a retirement corpus with regular contributions
  • A market-linked pension alongside fixed deposits

Getting your money back
Locked until 60 in most cases, with limited partial withdrawals after three years.
Protection
No capital protection: the corpus is invested in market-linked funds.
Tax
Contributions may qualify for a deduction. Part of the maturity amount is taxable; rules differ by how you exit.

What can go wrong

  • The corpus can fall: it is invested in equity and debt funds.
  • Part of the corpus must buy an annuity at exit, on terms available then.
  • Charges and fund choices affect the final corpus.

Before you decide anything

  • The fund manager and allocation you choose
  • The annuity rules at your exit age
