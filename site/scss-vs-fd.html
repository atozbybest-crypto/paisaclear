Senior Citizens' Savings Scheme: when it beats a fixed deposit

The highest rate available to most retirees is not automatically the best place for the money. Here is where it wins and where it ties your hands.

Reviewed 15 November 2026

SCSS pays 8.20%, notified for the quarter beginning 1 October 2026. A senior-citizen fixed deposit at a large bank typically pays 0.25% to 0.50% above its normal rate. On paper SCSS usually wins. After tax and after the practical restrictions, the answer depends on how much you need the money to stay put.

The arithmetic

SCSS pays interest quarterly and returns the capital at maturity after five years. The interest is taxable at your slab, and TDS applies above the threshold. At the 20% slab with cess, an 8.20% SCSS deposit keeps about 6.49%; at the 30% slab about 5.64%.

A senior FD at 7.75% keeps about 6.14% at 20% and 5.33% at 30%. On ₹20 lakh, the difference is roughly ₹7,000 to ₹12,000 a year — worth knowing, and worth checking against what your bank actually offers before you decide.

What SCSS gives you that a deposit does not

  • A government guarantee, not a bank one. Above ₹5 lakh, a bank deposit depends on the bank; SCSS depends on the sovereign.
  • A rate fixed for five years, notified when you invest. If rates fall, yours does not.
  • 80C eligibility on the deposit, in the old regime.
  • Extension by three years at maturity, with the rate then in force.

What it takes away

The money is locked. Premature closure is allowed after one year, with a penalty on the rate already paid. Close it in the first year and you may get back less than you put in after adjustment.

Interest is paid out, not compounded. That is deliberate — this is an income product — but it means the ₹20 lakh does not grow. If you do not need the quarterly income, you will need to reinvest it yourself, and the rate on that reinvestment is unknown today.

There is a ceiling. ₹30 lakh per depositor, including extensions. Above that, SCSS cannot hold your whole corpus.

The comparison nobody runs

Against PPF, SCSS pays more and is available only to those over 60. Against a bank FD, SCSS pays more and is safer above ₹5 lakh. Against a debt mutual fund, SCSS has no interest-rate risk on the capital and no expense ratio — but also no liquidity.

The honest way to compare is on the money you actually need. Split the corpus: five years of expenses that must never move, and the rest that can. SCSS is well suited to the part that must never move. It is a poor fit for money you may need for a medical bill or a family obligation next year.

The reinvestment problem, worked out

₹30 lakh at 8.20% pays ₹2.46 lakh a year, or about ₹20,500 a quarter. If you spend the interest, the capital stays at ₹30 lakh for five years while prices rise around it — an inflation problem, not a default risk.

If instead you reinvest each quarter into a recurring deposit at 6.5%, you end with roughly ₹2 lakh more than the untouched interest — a meaningful difference over five years, and the arithmetic most people never run. The catch is that you have to actually do it, every quarter, for five years.

Small practical points that catch people out

Open it at a post office or an authorised bank — the rate is the same. Bring proof of age, because the 60-year threshold is checked. If you are between 55 and 60 and retired on superannuation or under a VRS, you can open one within a month of receiving your retirement benefits, subject to conditions; that window is narrow and worth diarising.

The quarterly interest lands in your savings account. If TDS is deducted, file for a refund when you submit your return — many retirees overpay tax simply by not claiming it. Submit Form 15H if your total income is below the taxable limit and you want TDS stopped at source.

Who SCSS suits, and who it does not

It suits a retiree over 60 with a lump sum that must produce regular income and must not fall in value. Someone with no pension, or a pension that covers only part of monthly expenses. Someone who wants the money to be government-backed rather than bank-backed above ₹5 lakh.

It does not suit someone who may need the capital within five years — a house purchase, a family obligation, a medical contingency. It does not suit someone whose entire savings is under ₹10 lakh, because locking it all for five years removes the cushion that matters most. And it does not suit anyone still working and in the 30% slab, who cannot open one anyway.

SCSS against the three alternatives, side by side

Against a bank FD: higher rate, sovereign guarantee, but no premature access without penalty. The FD wins on flexibility, SCSS wins on rate and safety above ₹5 lakh.

Against PPF: SCSS pays more and pays it out; PPF compounds tax-free and stays liquid from year six. A couple can hold both — PPF for the spouse under 60, SCSS for the one over 60.

Against a debt mutual fund: SCSS fixes the rate for five years and returns the capital; a debt fund's NAV moves with rates and its return is not guaranteed. SCSS wins on certainty, the fund wins on liquidity and indexation after three years.

A worked plan for ₹40 lakh

Suppose a retiree of 63 has ₹40 lakh and monthly expenses of ₹35,000.

₹30 lakh into SCSS at 8.20% pays ₹20,500 a quarter — roughly ₹6,800 a month. Not enough alone, but it is the guaranteed floor.

₹6 lakh stays in a savings account and a sweep FD as the medical and emergency cushion — about eight months of expenses. This is the money that prevents an SCSS premature closure.

₹4 lakh goes into a short-duration debt fund or a ladder of FDs maturing each year, to cover the gap between ₹6,800 and ₹35,000 a month, topped up by any pension.

The point of the structure is not the returns. It is that no single event forces the locked money open.

Questions people ask

Can a husband and wife each open one? Yes. The ₹30 lakh cap is per depositor, so a couple can hold ₹60 lakh between them.

What happens on the depositor's death? The nominee receives the deposit and interest up to the date of death. No penalty applies.

Can I add money later? No. SCSS is a single deposit, unlike PPF. Additional money needs a second account within the cap.

Is the interest credited automatically? Yes, quarterly, to the linked savings account. If it is not being reinvested, it is being spent — plan for that deliberately.

What if rates rise after I open it? Your rate is fixed for the term. That is the trade for certainty, and it cuts both ways.

The tax filing detail that returns money

Interest from SCSS is taxable, and the post office or bank will deduct TDS above the threshold. If your total income is below the taxable limit — common for retirees living on interest and a small pension — submit Form 15H so no TDS is deducted at all. If TDS was already deducted, file the return and claim it back.

This is not a technicality. On ₹30 lakh at 8.20%, the annual interest is ₹2.46 lakh. A 10% TDS is ₹24,600 withheld each year — real money for a household on a fixed income, and routinely left unclaimed.

SCSS against the alternatives, in one table

Rate: SCSS 8.20% · PPF 7.10% · senior FD typically 7.25–7.75% · debt fund variable.
Guarantee: SCSS sovereign · PPF sovereign · FD bank (₹5 lakh insured) · fund none.
Payout: SCSS quarterly · PPF at maturity · FD quarterly or at maturity · fund at redemption.
Liquidity: SCSS after one year with penalty · PPF from year six · FD immediate with penalty · fund 1–2 days.
Tax: SCSS taxable · PPF exempt · FD taxable · fund taxed at slab or 12.5% LTCG.

The decision in one sentence

If you are over 60, need the income now, and will not touch the capital for five years, SCSS is the default — and the only question left is how much of your corpus should be locked rather than how much should be in SCSS.

Joint accounts and nomination

SCSS allows a joint account with a spouse, and the deposit is treated as belonging to the first holder for the ₹30 lakh cap. A nominee is mandatory in practice — without one, the balance goes through succession, which is slow and avoidable. Add the nominee at opening; changing it later requires a form and a visit.

If both spouses are over 60, two separate single accounts are usually cleaner than one joint account: each gets its own ₹30 lakh cap and its own quarterly payout.

The transition at 60

The year you turn 60, four things become available at once: SCSS, higher FD rates for seniors, a higher basic exemption limit, and in some cases a higher 80D deduction for health insurance. The efficient sequence is usually to move maturing FDs into SCSS up to the cap, keep a liquid cushion outside it, and review health cover before the next birthday makes it dearer.

The mistake is moving everything into SCSS in one go and then discovering that a medical bill or a family obligation needs money that is now locked for five years.
