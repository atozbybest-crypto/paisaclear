Ambuja Cements dividend history: the cut inside a 25-year record

Ambuja has paid every year since 2002, yet its dividend has fallen from ₹6.30 to ₹2.00. A streak counts years with a payment — it does not promise the amount holds.

What the record shows

Cement earnings move with construction demand, fuel costs and regional pricing power. Ambuja operates in the north and west, where capacity additions have been heavy — which is why the payout has flattened while plants are being built.

The clearest example on this site of why a streak is not a yield. In 2022 the company paid ₹6.30; since 2024 it has paid ₹2.00, flat. The record survived. The income fell by two thirds.

Cement is cyclical and the company has been through a change of control and a large capacity expansion. Dividends in this sector follow the capex cycle and the cement price cycle, not the length of the record.

The arithmetic

Across these 10 payments the company has paid a total of ₹37.80 per share — an average of ₹3.78 each time, with a low of ₹1.00 and a high of ₹17.00. On a holding of 100 shares, that is about ₹420 a year at the average rate, before tax.

What it does not tell you

A streak counts years in which something was paid. It says nothing about whether the next payment is safe. Two numbers decide that: the payout ratio against earnings, and free cash flow against the dividend. If the payout exceeds free cash flow for two years running, the record is being funded by the balance sheet.

Watch capacity expansion announcements. A year of heavy plant building usually means a flat or lower payout, whatever the streak says.

Tax

Dividends are taxed at your slab. The company deducts TDS above ₹5,000 in a year, and you claim the credit when you file. In the 30% slab a ₹1,000 dividend leaves about ₹688 — which is why comparing a dividend yield with a tax-free small-savings rate needs the post-tax number, not the headline.
