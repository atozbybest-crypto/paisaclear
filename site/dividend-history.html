Payment dates and amounts come from public dividend records; the streak is computed from those payments and states the window it was verified over on each company page. A long record is history, not a promise — a company can cut or skip a dividend in any year.

How to read a dividend record

Three things matter more than the streak itself. The direction: are the amounts rising, flat or slipping? A company paying ₹2, ₹2, ₹2.5, ₹3 over a decade is growing the payout; one paying ₹5, ₹5, ₹4, ₹3 is shrinking it while the record stays intact.

The cover: dividends are paid from free cash flow, not from the record. A payout ratio above 80% of earnings leaves no room for a bad year. The price: a 4% dividend on a share that fell 20% is not income, it is a smaller loss.

The full argument is in what a 25-year dividend streak actually predicts, and the tax treatment is in the after-tax comparison — dividends are taxed at your slab.
