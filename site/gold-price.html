What moves the rupee price

Three things, and only one of them is "gold" in the abstract. The dollar price of the metal moves with global demand, real interest rates and central-bank buying. The rupee moves independently: when the rupee weakens, the same dollar price buys fewer grams, so the Indian price rises even if gold does not move at all.

The third is duty and premium. India imports almost all of its gold, and the landed price carries customs duty plus whatever dealers are charging on the day. In a festive week the premium can widen by a percent or more on its own — which is why the figure at the top of this page and the number at a jeweller's counter are not the same number.

Physical gold, an ETF, or a bond

Physical gold carries making charges and a buy-sell spread that is often 5–10% round trip. A gold ETF tracks the metal with a small expense ratio and no locker, but you need a demat account and it trades in rupees like a share. Sovereign gold bonds paid interest and were redeemed at the metal price, but new tranches appear on the government's schedule rather than continuously.

Each has a different cost structure, and the differences are larger than most people assume over a decade. This page gives you the price; the choice is yours, and it depends on why you want the metal.

What this page is and is not

  • What it is: a dated, sourced record of gold in dollars, in rupees, month by month.
  • Not a jeweller's rate. No import duty, no premium, no making charges, no GST.
  • Not advice. Nothing here says whether gold is cheap or expensive.
  • Sources: gold futures and USD/INR from Yahoo Finance. Monthly candles are that month's own open, high, low and close.
