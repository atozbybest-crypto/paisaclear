Why the Indian price is higher

The figure above is a conversion, not a jeweller's quote. The landed Indian price adds import duty and a dealer premium, and jewellery adds making charges and GST on top. That is why your local rate is consistently above this number.

How this refreshes

The figures are pulled at 09:00 and 21:30 IST along with fund NAVs and bond yields, then the page is rebuilt. The date beside each figure is the last observation, not the day you are reading it.

What moves the rupee price

Three things, and only one of them is "gold" in the abstract. The dollar price of the metal moves with global demand, real interest rates and central-bank buying. The rupee moves independently: when the rupee weakens, the same dollar price buys fewer grams, so the Indian price rises even if gold does not move at all.

The third is duty and premium. India imports almost all of its gold, and the landed price carries customs duty plus whatever dealers are charging on the day. In a festive week the premium can widen by a percent or more on its own.

That is why the figure at the top of this page and the number at a jeweller's counter are different, and why comparing the two is not a like-for-like comparison.

Physical gold, an ETF, or a bond

Physical gold carries making charges and a buy-sell spread that is often 5–10% round trip. A gold ETF tracks the metal with a small expense ratio and no locker, but you need a demat account and it is priced in rupees like a share. Sovereign gold bonds paid interest and were redeemed at the metal price, but new tranches are issued on the government's schedule rather than continuously.

Each has a different cost structure, and the differences are larger than most people assume over a decade. This page gives you the price; the choice is yours, and it depends on why you want the metal at all.

What this page is and is not

  • What it is: a dated, sourced record of the gold price in dollars, in rupees, and through a listed ETF.
  • Not a jeweller's rate. No import duty, no premium, no making charges, no GST.
  • Not advice. Nothing here says whether gold is cheap or expensive, or whether to hold it.
  • Sources: gold futures and USD/INR from Yahoo Finance; ETF price from the NSE listing. Futures move continuously — this page is a snapshot with a date on it.
