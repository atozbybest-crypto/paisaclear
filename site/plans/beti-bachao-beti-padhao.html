Beti Bachao Beti Padhao

A national campaign for the survival, protection and education of the girl child. It is not a savings scheme — it pays no interest and accepts no deposit — so there is no calculator on this page. Here is what it actually does, and which scheme to use if you want to save for a daughter.

Government of India campaign · launched 22 January 2015

Not a money product. BBBP has no deposit, no interest rate and no maturity value. Any page that offers a "BBBP calculator" is showing you an illustration of something else.

What it is

A tri-ministerial effort launched at Panipat, Haryana, on 22 January 2015. The Ministry of Women and Child Development is the nodal ministry, working with the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare and the Ministry of Education. It is an implementation campaign — awareness, enforcement and delivery of existing services — not a financial instrument.

What it sets out to do

  • Prevent gender-biased sex selection, and improve the child sex ratio at birth.
  • Ensure the survival and protection of the girl child in the early years.
  • Ensure her education and participation — enrolment, retention and completion of schooling.
  • Change community attitudes through local campaigns, health workers and schools.

What it does not do

  • It does not pay you any money. There is no cash transfer under the campaign itself.
  • It does not open an account or give you a rate of return.
  • It does not have a per-family benefit you apply for at a counter.
  • Some states run their own girl-child cash schemes and use the BBBP name locally. Those are state schemes — check at your district office.

If you want to save for a daughter

The financial product built for that purpose is Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana — an account for a girl under ten, paying 8.20% a year (notified for the quarter beginning 2026-10-01), with deposits, interest and maturity all tax-free. It is the scheme that BBBP enrolment drives most often point families towards.

Where to verify

Campaign details, district-level implementation and state schemes are published by the Ministry of Women and Child Development. Rules and local benefits change, so confirm anything you plan to act on.
