Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana

A government savings account for a girl child, paying the highest small-savings rate and entirely tax-free. Here is exactly how it works, what the rules allow, and what it becomes over 21 years.

Who can open one

A parent or legal guardian opens the account for a girl child who is a resident Indian and under ten years old. One account per girl. A family can hold a maximum of two accounts — three if the second birth produced twins or triplets, so that the later daughters are not excluded.

The account is opened at a post office or an authorised bank with the child's birth certificate and the guardian's KYC. It is operated by the guardian until the girl turns 18.

How the money works

You deposit between ₹250 and ₹1.5 lakh in a financial year, for fifteen years. After that you stop depositing, but the balance keeps earning interest until the account matures at twenty-one years from opening. Interest is compounded annually and credited at the end of each year.

The rate is not fixed for the life of the account. It is notified every quarter, and the balance earns whatever rate is in force — the same rule as PPF. At 8.20% today, the projection below is what the account produces if that rate held for the whole term.

How it is taxed

  • Deposits qualify under Section 80C, up to ₹1.5 lakh — old regime only.
  • Interest is not taxed as it accrues.
  • Maturity proceeds are not taxed.

That combination is why the effective return beats a fixed deposit at a similar headline rate. An FD at 8.2% in the 30% slab nets roughly 5.7% after tax. SSY keeps the full 8.2%.

When you can take money out

  • For education — up to 50% of the balance at the end of the previous financial year, once she turns 18 or passes Class 10, whichever is earlier. A document showing admission or a fee notice is required.
  • For marriage — the account can be closed after she turns 18, subject to the rules in force. Check at the counter before relying on it.
  • Full closure before maturity is not normally allowed. Only the two cases above, or on the death of the account holder.
  • Missed a year? A ₹50 penalty per defaulted year applies, and the account can be revived by paying the minimum plus the penalty.

SSY or PPF?

SSY pays more — 8.20% against 7.10% — and has the same tax treatment. If you have a daughter under ten, it is the better home for that money. PPF is the option when you do not, or when you need the flexibility of partial withdrawal from year six.
