PPF vs FD vs NSC

Three safe places for long-term money, and they are not interchangeable. What each promises, how long it locks the money, and how it is taxed.

Official rates as of 2026-10-01

PPFBank FDNSC
Rate7.10% a year, notified quarterlySet by each bank, varies by tenure7.70% a year, notified quarterly
Who paysGovernment of IndiaThe bank, insured up to the DICGC limitGovernment of India
Term15 years, extendable in 5-year blocks7 days to 10 years, typically5 years
AccessPartial withdrawal after 5 years, with conditionsBreakable any time, usually at a penaltyLocked for 5 years

Section 80CYesOnly the 5-year tax-saver FDYes
Best fitRetirement, long compoundingMoney needed at a known dateA 5-year block with 80C

The tax difference is bigger than the rate difference

PPF interest is exempt at all three stages — you get a deduction on what you put in, the interest is not taxed as it builds, and the maturity amount is not taxed. An FD paying a higher headline rate can still leave you with less, because its interest is taxed at your slab every year.

At a 30% slab, an FD needs to pay roughly 10.1% to match a 7.10% tax-free PPF. No bank FD currently pays that. For anyone in the 20% or 30% bracket, PPF wins on after-tax return unless the money must stay reachable.

When an FD is the right answer anyway

  • You need the money in two or three years. PPF's 15-year term and 5-year lock make it unsuitable for that.
  • You want a fixed rate for a known period. PPF's rate is re-notified every quarter.
  • You are in a low tax bracket, where the tax drag is small.

What NSC adds

NSC sits between the two: a government-backed 5-year certificate at 7.70%, with 80C on the investment, but the interest is taxable. It suits someone who wants a five-year block and has 80C room left after PPF.

Rates: 7.10% for PPF and 7.70% for NSC, notified for the quarter beginning 2026-10-01 — National Savings Institute. Bank FD rates vary by bank and tenure, so each bank's record links to its own rate card.
