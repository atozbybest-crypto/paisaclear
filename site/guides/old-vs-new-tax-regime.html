Old vs new tax regime

The new regime is the default and has lower slabs, but almost no deductions. Here is the trade-off, with the numbers behind it.

A worked example

Take a ₹15,00,000 salary. Under the new regime, taxable income after the ₹75,000 standard deduction is ₹14,25,000, and the tax comes to about ₹97,500 including cess.

Under the old regime the same salary needs deductions to compete. With ₹1.5 lakh of 80C, ₹1.5 lakh of HRA exemption, ₹25,000 of 80D and ₹25,000 of home-loan interest — ₹3.5 lakh in total — taxable income falls to ₹11,00,000 and the tax is about ₹1,48,200. The new regime is still ahead by roughly ₹50,000.

The old regime only pulls ahead when the deductions are large: a big HRA claim in a metro city, a large home loan, and the full 80C. For most salaried people without those, the new regime wins despite the higher-sounding deductions.

What the old regime still allows

  • HRA exemption, if you pay rent — usually the biggest single item
  • 80C up to ₹1.5 lakh: PPF, ELSS, life insurance, principal on a home loan
  • 80D health insurance, 24(b) home-loan interest, 80E education-loan interest
  • 80CCD(1B) — an extra ₹50,000 for NPS, still available under the new regime too
