NPS vs PPF

PPF is a guaranteed, tax-free account. NPS is a market-linked pension with a lock-in until 60. They are not substitutes.

PPF rate as of 2026-10-01

National Pension SystemPPF
ReturnMarket-linked, no guarantee7.10%, notified quarterly
Lock-inUntil 60 (Tier I)15 years, partial access from year 6
Tax on exit60% of the corpus is tax-free, 40% must buy an annuityEntirely tax-free
Extra deduction80CCD(1B): ₹50,000 beyond 80C, in both regimesWithin the ₹1.5 lakh 80C cap, old regime only
RiskYoursNone beyond the government's own

NPS exists for a specific reason: it is the only route to an extra ₹50,000 deduction under 80CCD(1B), and it stays available under the new regime. That is a real benefit if you are in the 30% bracket.

But 40% of the corpus must buy an annuity at 60, and the annuity rate is whatever it is that day. PPF gives you a smaller, certain, entirely tax-free amount with no forced purchase at the end.

Rules per the PFRDA and the Income-tax Act; PPF rate from the National Savings Institute, quarter beginning 2026-10-01.
