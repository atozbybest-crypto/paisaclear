# "NOBODY TOLD ME WHEN" — PaisaClear brand film

**Length:** 60s master · 20s cutdown · 10s bumper
**Language:** Hindi VO, English supers (or English-only variant below)
**Tone:** quiet, observational, warm. Not a warning. Not a lecture. A person figuring something out.

---

## Logline

A woman saving for her daughter's education collects advice from everyone she trusts. Every number is
sincere. Every number is out of date. She finds out too late that none of them carried a date — and
decides to check for herself.

---

## Why this works

Every finance ad in India sells *more*: more return, more safety, more growth. This one sells **the truth
about a number**, which is the only thing PaisaClear can honestly own. It also does the one thing ads
rarely do — it makes the viewer feel clever rather than scared.

The villain is not a bank, an insurer or a scammer. It is **a number without a date**. That is a villain
you can defeat in nine seconds with a website, which is exactly what the ad needs to prove.

---

## Cast

**MEERA**, 34. Works in a private company. Not glamorous, not poor. Dressed for a normal Tuesday.
Her face does the work; she never cries, never shouts. The most emotional she gets is a small, tired
exhale at 0:22.

**Her daughter, AANYA**, 9. Present but never the subject of a shot on her own — she is the reason,
not the story.

---

## Art direction

- **Palette:** warm lamp, off-white paper, one deep green (the brand green) appearing only twice — once
  on the laptop screen, once on the end card. Nothing else competes.
- **Light:** late evening, one practical lamp, window light dying. No studio gloss.
- **Props:** real paper. A crumpled bank pamphlet, a school fee letter, a phone with a WhatsApp forward,
  a notebook with numbers crossed out. All handwritten marks — nothing printed twice.
- **Camera:** locked-off or very slow push. No whip pans. Handheld only in the memory cut.
- **Typography:** the site's own serif, appearing as if printed on the paper itself, not as a graphic
  overlay.

## THE FILM — 60 seconds

### 0:00 – 0:08 — "Everyone had a number for me."

**VISUAL:** Extreme close-up. Two hands flatten a crumpled bank pamphlet on a wooden table. The paper
will not sit flat. A printed figure is in focus: **8.50%**.

**CAMERA:** Slow push in, 6 seconds.

**SOUND:** Room tone only. A single piano note, struck once, left to decay.

**VO (female, low, unhurried):**
> "Everyone had a number for me."

**SUPER:** none.

---

### 0:08 – 0:20 — "My bank said one thing. My cousin said another."

**VISUAL:** Three cuts, each 3–4 seconds, all top-down on the same table.

1. The pamphlet — **8.50%**
2. A phone screen, a family WhatsApp forward — **"PPF 8.7% hai, maine karaya tha"**
3. A notebook, a figure in blue ink — **9.10%** — circled twice

**CAMERA:** Each shot locked off. The three papers overlap slightly in frame, so the viewer feels the
pile growing.

**SOUND:** The piano note repeats, same note, once per cut. A chair creaks.

**VO:**
> "My bank said one thing. My cousin said another. My colleague sent a forward."

**SUPER:** none.

---

### 0:20 – 0:28 — The exhale

**VISUAL:** MEERA, medium shot, sitting at the table. She looks at the three papers. She exhales — small,
tired, almost a laugh. She picks up a pen.

**CAMERA:** Static. Let her breathe. This is the only shot of her face in the first half.

**SOUND:** Pen cap. The piano stops.

**VO:**
> "They were all telling the truth."

**SUPER:** none.

---

### 0:28 – 0:38 — The turn

**VISUAL:** She turns each paper over. On the back of the pamphlet, in small print: **"rates as on 01.04.2021"**.
On the WhatsApp screenshot, a date: **2022**. On the notebook page, the corner: **2023**.

Three dates. All different. All old.

**CAMERA:** One continuous move across the three papers — the only camera movement in the film that
reveals something.

**SOUND:** A soft, low string enters underneath. Barely there.

**VO:**
> "They just never told me when."

**SUPER (small, bottom left, appears with the third date):**
> *Not one of them carried a date.*

---

### 0:38 – 0:50 — The answer

**VISUAL:** She opens a laptop. The screen fills the frame — no logo yet, just the site, a rate with a
date beside it, and a link underneath: **"Source ↗"**. Her finger scrolls once. The date stays in view.

**CAMERA:** Over-shoulder, then a cut to the screen itself.

**SOUND:** The string resolves into a single warm chord.

**VO:**
> "A rate without a date isn't a fact. It's a guess."

**SUPER, tracking the on-screen date:**
> **8.20%** — as of 1 October 2026

---

### 0:50 – 0:60 — End card

**VISUAL:** She closes the laptop. The room is a little darker. She looks up — not at the camera, at the
doorway where her daughter is standing, out of focus. Small smile. Cut to green.

**SOUND:** Chord decays. Room tone. Nothing else.

**VO:**
> "PaisaClear. Every figure dated. Every figure sourced. Before you put money in."

**END CARD (deep green, paper-white serif):**

> **PaisaClear**
> Understand the product before you put money in it.
> **paisaclear.co.in**
> *Free. No sign-up. No advice.*

## Production notes

**Do not add a voice that sounds like a salesman.** The single biggest risk is a bright, "subscribe now"
read. The VO must sound like a friend telling you something they just learned.

**Do not show a phone number, a bank logo, or a competitor's product.** The moment a brand appears, the
film becomes an attack ad and loses its authority.

**Do not show money.** No bundles of cash, no coins, no graphs going up. The film is about paper and
dates, not wealth.

**Music:** one piano note, one string, one chord. If you can hum it after watching, it is too much.

**Where the brand appears:** twice, briefly, in the last twelve seconds. Never before.

---

## What the viewer should feel at 0:38

Not fear — **recognition**. "That's me. I've done that." The sale happens in that half-second, before
the website appears. The website is only there to answer the question the film has just created.
